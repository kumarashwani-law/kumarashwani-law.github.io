Your account or page was blocked. What can you do?

A step-by-step guide for Indian users, creators and businesses whose Instagram, Facebook, X or YouTube account or post has been blocked: who blocked it, how to appeal, and when courts can help.

In short

  • First work out who blocked you. A block by the platform and a block ordered by the government have different remedies.
  • Platform block: appeal in the app, then complain to the platform's Grievance Officer, then appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee (GAC) within 30 days. After that, a civil suit.
  • Government block: ask for the order, and challenge it in the High Court by writ petition.
  • Save everything from day one. Screenshots, emails, dates and your losses are your evidence at every stage.

Step 1: Save the evidence

Before you do anything else:

  1. Screenshot the block or suspension message, with the date and time visible.
  2. Save every email or in-app notice the platform sends you.
  3. Note your account name, URL, follower count and, if you earn from the account, what you're losing each week.
  4. If you can still log in, download your data (most platforms have a "Download your information" option).

Step 2: Who blocked you?

Who blocked it Usual signs Main route
The platform itself Message says it broke "community standards", spam, impersonation or a copyright complaint In-app appeal → Grievance Officer → GAC → civil suit
The government Message says "withheld in India", "in response to a legal demand" or a government notice Ask for the order → writ petition in the High Court

The problem is that platform messages often don't say which it is. If you can't tell, ask the platform's Grievance Officer directly, in writing: was this action taken under your own policies, or on a government order or notice?

If the platform blocked you

  1. Appeal inside the app straight away. Use the "Request review" or "Appeal" option. Keep the reference number.
  2. Complain to the Grievance Officer. Every platform must publish a Grievance Officer's contact details (usually in its Help Centre or legal pages). Under the IT Rules, 2021, the Grievance Officer has to acknowledge your complaint within 24 hours and resolve it within 15 days.
  3. Appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee. If the Grievance Officer rejects you or doesn't reply, appeal online at gac.gov.in. You can appeal within 30 days of the Grievance Officer's decision, or of getting no response. The GAC is meant to try to decide appeals within 30 days. In practice it is badly backlogged: as of 22 September 2026, 49,978 of 63,353 appeals were pending.
  4. Use your right to reasons. Large platforms (significant social media intermediaries) must, under Rule 4(8) of the IT Rules, notify you with reasons and give you a fair chance to dispute when they remove content or disable access on their own. Quote Rule 4(8) in your complaints.
  5. Civil suit, if needed. If none of this works, the remedy is a civil suit for restoration (and damages if you can prove loss). You can't usually go straight to the High Court by writ against the platform: in Sarthak Bhagat v. Union of India (27 October 2026), the Delhi High Court held that Meta doesn't perform a public function, so a writ isn't maintainable against it.

Can the platform make you sue in California? Not necessarily. In Meta Platforms v. Blink Digital (27 October 2026), the Bombay High Court refused to send an Indian advertiser's suit to California despite Meta's terms, because it raised duties under the IT Act and IT Rules that an Indian court has to decide.

If it was a copyright complaint

Platforms may act on copyright complaints, and can suspend accounts. In Anmol Jain v. Union of India (17 September 2026), the Madhya Pradesh High Court upheld this, treating the Grievance Officer and GAC as adequate safeguards. But you can still:

  • ask for the complaint itself: who claimed it, and which work they say you copied;
  • argue fair dealing (memes, commentary and reviews may qualify under section 52 of the Copyright Act);
  • under the Copyright Rules, 2013 (Rule 75), the complainant has to identify the work and their ownership, and if no court order follows within 21 days the content may be restored.

If the government ordered the block

There are two main legal routes the government uses:

  • Section 69A of the IT Act with the 2009 Blocking Rules. These give the originator notice and a hearing where possible.
  • Section 79(3)(b) notices to the platform. Since the 2025 amendment to Rule 3(1)(d), these can only be issued by senior, specifically authorised officers (at least DIG rank for police), in writing, with reasons and exact URLs.

What to do:

  1. Ask for the order. Write to the platform's Grievance Officer asking for a copy of the government order or notice, and who issued it. You can also file an RTI application with the department you suspect.
  2. Check it. Was it issued by an officer with power to issue it? Does it give reasons? Does it name specific posts, or the whole page?
  3. Go to the High Court. A government block can be challenged by writ petition. In Haryana 24 News Network v. Union of India (reported 20 October 2026), the Punjab & Haryana High Court held that a notice issued by a Superintendent of Police was without jurisdiction, that a copy must go to the user too, and ordered the page restored within 24 hours.

Checklist

  • ☐ Screenshots of the block notice, with date and time
  • ☐ In-app appeal filed; reference number saved
  • ☐ Written complaint to the Grievance Officer; date noted
  • ☐ GAC appeal filed within 30 days (calendar reminder set)
  • ☐ Written request asking whether a government order was involved
  • ☐ Record of lost income or business
  • ☐ Backup of your content and followers list

Common questions

Can I complain to the police? Only if a crime is involved, for example if someone hacked your account or is impersonating you. A platform's policy decision isn't a police matter.

Will a lawyer's notice help? Sometimes. A notice citing Rule 4(8) and asking for reasons and restoration can prompt a review, and it is useful evidence if you later sue.

My account was hacked, not blocked. That's different: use the platform's hacked-account recovery process, and report the hacking at cybercrime.gov.in or on 1930.
