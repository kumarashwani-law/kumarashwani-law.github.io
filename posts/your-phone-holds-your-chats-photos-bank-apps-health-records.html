Your phone holds your chats, photos, bank apps, health records and contacts. Who can unlock it and copy everything, and under what law?

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 11 November 2026
View on LinkedIn

Documents made public by the Internet Freedom Foundation on 10 November show the Home Minister approved a ₹4.98 crore Cellebrite tool for the NIA in January, with a one-time exemption from "Make in India" procurement rules. The specification: full extraction from Android phones and iPhones, including encrypted devices, with brute-force passcode attempts.

It's not just the NIA. IFF counts 15 listings naming Cellebrite products in 2026 government tenders, from SFIO and Kerala's Home Department to Karnataka Police. Delhi Police has had such tools since at least 2020. (Purchase records show procurement, not confirmed use on anyone.)

The legal gap:
🔹 BNSS ss. 105–106 cover search, seizure and video recording, not unlocking or copying a phone
🔹 IT Act s. 69 needs a written Home Secretary order to decrypt, and has been under challenge in the Supreme Court since 2019
🔹 BSA s. 63 certifies electronic records for court; it doesn't make the extraction lawful
🔹 DPDP Act s. 17 exempts processing for investigation (from May 2027)
🔹 Guidelines on seizing digital devices, promised to the Supreme Court in 2023, still haven't been placed before it

Under Puttaswamy, any intrusion on privacy needs a law, a legitimate aim and proportionality. Investigating crime is a legitimate aim. But there's no specific law, and full extraction copies far more than any offence needs.

If your phone is seized (slide 6):
✔ Get the seizure memo listing the device
✔ Ask that the hash value be recorded at seizure
✔ Take legal advice before giving passwords; the law on compelled passwords is unsettled
✔ Lawyers and journalists: flag privileged and source material in writing
✔ Apply for return of the device (BNSS s. 497) and deletion of copied data if you're not charged

My take: investigators need these tools, but the rules should come before the purchase orders. A judicial warrant for extraction, scope limited to the offence, a log of every extraction, deletion when no charge is filed, independent audits, and extra protection for lawyers and journalists.
