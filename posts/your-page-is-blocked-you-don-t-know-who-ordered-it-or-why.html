Your page is blocked. You don't know who ordered it, or why. How do you challenge that?

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 27 October 2026 · 3 min read
View on LinkedIn

Two High Courts dealt with exactly this in the past week.

🔹 Punjab & Haryana HC (Haryana 24 News Network v. Union of India, reported 20 October): a Superintendent of Police sent Meta a notice under Section 79(3)(b) of the IT Act, and a news company's Facebook page was blocked. Justice Tribhuvan Dahiya held:
• The notice was without jurisdiction: Rule 3(1)(d) requires an officer of at least DIG rank, specifically authorised
• When a notice goes to the platform, a copy must go to the user too
• The user needn't first use Meta's grievance process; without the grounds, there's nothing to answer
The page was ordered restored within 24 hours.

🔹 Kerala HC (Hyder Ali K. v. Union of India, 26 October): Malayalam news outlet NoCap's Facebook page was restricted on 28 September after a "government notice" it has never seen. The court asked the Centre and the State whether any order was sent to Meta, and listed the case for 5 November.

Why this keeps happening:
Section 69A and the 2009 Blocking Rules give the originator notice and a hearing where possible. That's why Shreya Singhal upheld them. But many takedowns now go through Section 79(3)(b): a notice to the platform, which loses safe harbour if it doesn't comply. The 2025 amendment to Rule 3(1)(d) added real safeguards (senior officers only, written reasons, exact URLs, monthly review). It still says nothing about telling the user.

My take: you can't challenge an order you can't see.
1️⃣ Write user notice into Rule 3(1)(d), so it applies across India, not just in one High Court's territory
2️⃣ Give the user the reasons, with narrow exceptions recorded in writing
3️⃣ Where urgency rules out prior notice, give a hearing within a fixed time after the block
4️⃣ Publish figures from the monthly reviews
5️⃣ Block the item, not the whole page, wherever possible

If your page or account is blocked (slide 7): save the platform's message, ask for the order (grievance officer, RTI), test it against Rule 3(1)(d), record your losses, and move the High Court early.
