Meta's terms said California. The Bombay High Court said Mumbai.

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 2 November 2026
View on LinkedIn

In August, Meta disabled Mumbai ad agency Blink Digital's 4 business manager accounts and about 9,000 linked ad accounts. Meta says Blink allowed scam and sexual-services ads; Blink denies this and says it got no notice or hearing. Blink sued for restoration and damages of over ₹1,000 crore.

Meta's first move: send the case to California. Its Commercial Terms choose California courts and California law.

On 27 October, Justice Sandeep V. Marne refused (Meta Platforms v. Blink Digital):
🔹 A private contract can't override Indian law. The suit alleges breaches of the IT Act and IT Rules, 2021, which a California court applying only Meta's terms and California law can't decide
🔹 Facebook India, with a Mumbai office, is a defendant and didn't object to jurisdiction; Meta couldn't object on its behalf
🔹 The claims are interconnected, so the suit can't be split

Why it matters:
Meta relies on Rule 3(1)(c), which lets platforms end access for policy breaches. Blink relies on Rule 4(8): when a significant social media intermediary removes content or disables access on its own, it must first notify the user with reasons and allow a fair chance to dispute. If the California clause had won, that right would be close to unenforceable for Indian businesses.

Read it carefully: this decides only Meta's application, and jurisdiction can still be argued at trial. There's no finding on whether the ads were unlawful or the shutdown fair. Indian courts generally respect foreign jurisdiction clauses between commercial parties; here, Indian statutory duties and an Indian defendant tipped the balance.

My take:
1️⃣ Right result: Indian statutory duties need an Indian forum
2️⃣ Platforms must still fight scams fast; due process and fraud prevention aren't opposites
3️⃣ Disabling 9,000 accounts at once should need clear reasons and a quick review route

For agencies, brands and creators (slide 7): read the platform's terms (forum, law, termination), vet advertisers and keep records, back up data and access, demand written reasons under Rule 4(8), ask the platform to preserve your data, and appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee within 30 days.
