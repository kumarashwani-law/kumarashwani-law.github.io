Did AI help draft your trademark filing? Under new draft rules, you'll have to say so.

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 21 October 2026
View on LinkedIn

India's trademark office (O/o CGPDTM) has published draft Guidelines on Use of Artificial Intelligence in Proceedings before the Trade Marks Registry, 2026. The Delhi High Court directed them in Stryten Energy LLC v. Nidhi Agarwal (W.P.(C)-IPD 16/2026) after AI-drafted pleadings cited case law that doesn't exist.

What the draft requires:

1️⃣ Disclosure in every filing
Every submission must carry a declaration (Annexure I) stating whether AI was used. If it was, you confirm that every citation, rule and fact was independently verified. Not disclosing is treated as misrepresentation.

2️⃣ A 4-point citation test
Before citing any case, confirm that:
• it exists and was decided by the court named
• the citation details are correct
• the holding attributed to it is accurate
• it is relevant
And read the full judgment from a non-AI source: court websites, the Gazette, SCC Online, Manupatra.

3️⃣ No AI-made evidence
AI cannot change the substance of affidavits, or fabricate or alter labels, invoices, sales data, surveys, website extracts, translations or proof of use. If authenticity is doubted, the Registrar can demand the native file, metadata, audit trails and server logs, and draw adverse inferences.

4️⃣ Real consequences
After a show-cause notice and hearing, the Registrar can strike out the citation or the whole submission. Agents can be sent to a disciplinary committee and advocates to the Bar Council. Anyone can report a suspected fake citation (Annexure II), but frivolous reports can be penalised too.

The principle running through it: "Final decision-making authority … shall rest at all times with the submitting human." Using AI is no defence.

Why it matters: courts have been sanctioning fake AI citations case by case. This is one of the first times an Indian regulator has written down rules for people who appear before it. The patent office issued AI guidelines for its own examiners in August; this draft goes further and covers applicants, agents and advocates.

My checklist for IP practitioners (slide 8):
✔ Keep an AI-use log for each matter
✔ Verify every citation against the full judgment
✔ Keep native files and metadata of all evidence
✔ Draft affidavits without AI
✔ Keep client documents out of public AI tools

The draft is not yet in force and takes effect once notified.
