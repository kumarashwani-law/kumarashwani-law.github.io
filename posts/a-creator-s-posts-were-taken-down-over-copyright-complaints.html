A creator's posts were taken down over copyright complaints. Then his whole Instagram account was suspended. No notice, no hearing. The High Court says that's within the rules.

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 28 October 2026
View on LinkedIn

In Anmol Jain v. Union of India (order dated 17 September, reported 27 October), the Madhya Pradesh High Court dismissed a meme creator's challenge to Rule 3(1)(b)(iv) of the IT Rules, 2021. His page had about 39,000 followers and was a source of income. He argued that a private company shouldn't decide copyright infringement; only courts should.

The court held:
🔹 Instagram is an intermediary under s. 2(w) of the IT Act, with due-diligence duties that include acting against infringing content
🔹 Once the law lets a private company operate as an intermediary, it may manage and control its platform
🔹 The grievance officer (Rule 3(2)) and the Grievance Appellate Committee (Rule 3A) are adequate safeguards

What it means for creators, in order:
1️⃣ Appeal in-app straight away
2️⃣ Grievance Officer: must acknowledge in 24 hours and resolve in 15 days
3️⃣ Grievance Appellate Committee: appeal online at gac.gov.in within 30 days
4️⃣ Then other legal routes, including action against a bad-faith claimant

The gap: the Copyright Rules, 2013 (Rule 75) build in a check. The complainant must identify the work and its ownership, and if no court order follows within 21 days, the content may be restored. In practice, platform policy decides, at scale, and creators often never see the complaint. Memes and reviews may be fair dealing under s. 52(1)(a), but a platform team makes that call, not a judge.

My take: the court is right that platforms must act on infringement. But the process needs:
✔ Notice to the creator: the complaint, the claimant and the work said to be copied
✔ Proportionality: remove the post first; suspend accounts only for repeat infringement, after warnings
✔ Checks on false claims used to silence rivals or critics
✔ Published GAC decisions, and platform compliance with them

Checklist for creators and brands (slide 7): use licensed music and clips, keep licences and original files, screenshot every notice, track the 30-day GAC deadline, record lost income, and back up your content.

On someone else's platform, your rights are mostly procedural. Use every step.
