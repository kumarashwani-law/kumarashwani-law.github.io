Three weeks ago I wrote about courts protecting actors from AI deepfakes. Now the Delhi High Court has given "personality rights" a reality check.

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 9 November 2026 · 2 min read
View on LinkedIn

In Kriti Sanon v. BBE Marketing Inc & Ors, Justice Purushaindra Kumar Kaurav protected the actor, for now, against AI chatbots and voice models posing as her, sexual deepfakes and impersonating accounts. Satire, parody, reviews and fan content are expressly carved out.

The reasoning is what matters:
🔹 "Personality rights" is an umbrella, not a single cause of action. It covers publicity, passing off, privacy, defamation, and trade mark or copyright rights, including performers' rights
🔹 Each claim keeps its own test. Bundling them doesn't lower the bar, especially for restraining speech before trial
🔹 Mere use of someone's identity isn't unlawful by itself
🔹 "The rights are available to every individual." Fame affects only commercial value and loss

So plaintiffs must now show which claim, against which defendant, for which content, rather than seeking one blanket injunction.

The same week, the same judge decreed Amitabh Bachchan's suit (5 November), extending it to unknown infringers because the plaint proved a continuing pattern of anonymous misuse. Wide relief is still possible when the evidence supports it.

If it happens to you (slide 6):
✔ Morphed or sexual deepfake: report to the platform, which must remove it within 2 hours under the IT Rules 2026, and file at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930
✔ Fake account posing as you: platform impersonation report; police complaint if money is sought
✔ Your face or voice selling a product: legal notice, then a suit for passing off and publicity
✔ Fake content harming your reputation: notice, takedown, defamation suit

My take: this is a needed correction. A catch-all "right" risked chilling satire, reviews and fan art. But claim-by-claim pleading costs time and money most victims don't have. For ordinary people, fast platform remedies matter more than celebrity suits, and Parliament should define these rights clearly.
