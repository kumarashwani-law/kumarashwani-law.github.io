Hyderabad Police now has an AI that maps "criminal networks". Which law tells it where to stop?

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 14 November 2026 · 2 min read
View on LinkedIn

On 12 November, Commissioner V.C. Sajjanar launched Dooradrishti, an AI platform built in-house by Hyderabad City Police. It compiles profiles of accused persons, analyses their activities and identifies links between individuals and criminal networks, using police databases and data from other departments. The stated aim: faster evidence, faster charge sheets, and a shift "from reactive to proactive policing".

Organised crime and cyber fraud do run through networks, so the aim is legitimate. But five questions haven't been answered publicly:
1️⃣ Does it include people never convicted, or acquitted?
2️⃣ How does it make links, and how are false links caught?
3️⃣ Does it reach beyond criminal records, to movements or activity?
4️⃣ Who can access it, for how long, and is it audited?
5️⃣ Must officers verify its findings before acting?

The legal frame:
🔹 Puttaswamy: State intrusion on privacy needs a law, a legitimate aim, proportionality and safeguards
🔹 DPDP Act s. 17: processing to prevent, detect or investigate offences is largely exempt (from May 2027)
🔹 Malak Singh (1981): police surveillance registers must be limited to people reasonably suspected
🔹 Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022: records of people never convicted must be destroyed on release, discharge or acquittal

And Telangana has been here before. In 2022, the High Court issued notice in S.Q. Masood's PIL, India's first court challenge to police facial recognition without a law.

The risk with network mapping is guilt by link: a relative, neighbour or client flagged by association, old FIRs and acquittals that never leave the system, and no way to know or correct your profile. Being linked to someone isn't evidence of a crime.

My take: six safeguards before scale (slide 7):
✔ A published legal basis and SOP
✔ Scope limited to charge-sheeted and convicted persons, for specific cases
✔ Human verification: AI output is a lead, never evidence on its own
✔ Deletion for the acquitted and discharged
✔ Logs and independent audits of every search
✔ A right to know and correct one's profile, with an annual transparency report

Hyderabad can lead on AI policing. It should also lead on the rules.
