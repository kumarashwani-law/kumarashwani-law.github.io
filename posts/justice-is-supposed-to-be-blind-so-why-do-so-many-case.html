Justice is supposed to be blind. So why do so many case papers still begin with a litigant's caste?

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 5 November 2026
View on LinkedIn

In Shama Sharma v. Kishan Kumar (order dated 10 January 2024), the Supreme Court (Justices Hima Kohli and Ahsanuddin Amanullah) directed that the caste or religion of litigants must not appear in the memo of parties, in the Supreme Court, in every High Court and in every subordinate court. Even if the lower court's papers carried it. In the Court's words, the practice "is to be shunned and must be ceased forthwith."

High Courts issued circulars to follow it. In 2025, the Allahabad High Court went further and barred caste of accused and witnesses in FIRs and police records, which Uttar Pradesh implemented, except for SC/ST Act cases.

Yet the habit lingers in filings across the country.

My take: a caste label on the first page of a case does real damage.
1️⃣ It erodes the image of an unbiased system. A court that labels litigants by caste signals that identity matters, even when it doesn't
2️⃣ It invites bias. Identity is on the page before the facts are, and prejudice, conscious or not, can follow
3️⃣ It can be used against marginalised litigants, turning a legal dispute into a social one
4️⃣ It disrupts the process. Arguments drift from merits to identity

The balance: where caste is the legal issue, such as offences under the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act or reservation and caste-certificate disputes, it belongs in the pleadings as a fact, argued on evidence. The rule is about how a litigant is identified, not about hiding relevant facts.

For advocates (slide 6):
✔ Drop caste and religion from the memo of parties, even if the lower court had them
✔ Name, parentage, age, occupation and address are enough to identify a party
✔ Plead caste only where it's an ingredient of the case
✔ If the registry objects, cite Shama Sharma and your High Court's circular

A litigant is a party, not a caste. Equality before law under Article 14 starts with the first page of the case.
