Instagram blocked your account. Can you go straight to the High Court? The Delhi High Court says no, at least not by writ.

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 6 November 2026
View on LinkedIn

In Sarthak Bhagat v. Union of India (order dated 27 October), Justice Amit Mahajan dealt with a user whose Instagram accounts were disabled without notice or specific reasons. The in-app review failed, and his appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee (GAC) went undecided past its 30-day deadline.

The court held:
🔹 A writ under Article 226 isn't maintainable against Meta. It doesn't perform a public function or discharge a public duty
🔹 Instagram shapes public opinion, but that's a consequence of the platform, not a duty it owes. Its operations "remain private in nature"
🔹 Restoration has to be sought through a civil suit
🔹 The GAC was directed to decide his appeal quickly

The catch: the GAC is jammed. As of 22 September, 49,978 of 63,353 appeals were pending, about four in five.

How it fits with three other High Court rulings in six weeks:
• MP HC (Anmol Jain, 17 Sept): platforms can act on infringing content; GAC is an adequate safeguard
• P&H HC (reported 20 Oct): government-ordered blocks can be challenged by writ
• Bombay HC (Meta v. Blink Digital, 27 Oct): Indian civil courts can hear IT Rules claims, despite Meta's California clause
• Delhi HC (27 Oct): no writ for Meta's own decisions

So the first question is: who blocked you?
✔ The platform (community standards, copyright, spam): in-app appeal → Grievance Officer → GAC within 30 days → civil suit, relying on Rule 4(8)'s notice-and-reasons duty
✔ The government (s. 69A or s. 79(3)(b), including via Sahyog): ask for the order → writ against the State

The problem: platform notices often don't say which kind of block it is, or who ordered it.

My take: the remedy works on paper only. The GAC needs more benches and an enforced 30-day deadline. Every notice should say whether the platform or the government ordered the block. And small creators need a fast interim route; a full civil suit is too slow and costly for most of them.
