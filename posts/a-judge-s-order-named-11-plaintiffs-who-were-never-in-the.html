A judge's order named 11 plaintiffs who were never in the case. A US appeals court has now taken the case away from him.

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 25 October 2026
View on LinkedIn

On 22 October, the US Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit vacated an injunction against Mississippi's anti-DEI law and ordered the case reassigned to another judge (Jackson Federation of Teachers v. Fitch).

What went wrong:
🔹 The July 2025 order named parties who were never in the case, quoted words not in the statute and relied on declarants who may not exist
🔹 Once the State flagged it, the order was replaced with a "corrected" version dated the same day, and the errors called "clerical"
🔹 The corrected version still cited a case that doesn't exist
🔹 The judge later said a law clerk had used an AI tool on a draft

Judge Jerry Smith called it "a botch job, to say the least."

This isn't the first time. A New Jersey federal judge withdrew an opinion after an intern used ChatGPT, and a Georgia appeals court vacated an order built on fake cases from a lawyer's draft. Lawyers get sanctioned for fake citations; judges, so far, have only seen their orders set aside.

Where India stands:
✔ Kerala High Court policy (July 2025): AI can't be used for findings, orders or judgments; every output must be verified and AI use recorded
✔ Supreme Court's draft Regulations for Use of AI in Courts, 2026: no outcome by algorithm alone (Reg. 20), and accountability rests "exclusively" on the officer; hallucinations are no excuse (Reg. 8(1))
But the draft lets verification be skipped for recorded reasons (Reg. 8(3)), and it still has to be notified.

My take, five fixes:
1️⃣ No exceptions: every citation and quotation in an order must be checked
2️⃣ Disclosure should cover AI help in drafting orders, not just administration
3️⃣ Chambers protocol: researcher drafts, a second person checks sources, judge signs
4️⃣ Errors corrected by a recorded order, never a silent or backdated replacement
5️⃣ Notify the regulations across all High Courts

If an order you receive looks AI-tainted (slide 7): check every citation and quote against the source, map each error in a table, act before limitation runs out, and attack the error, never the judge. And verify your own drafts; courts sometimes adopt them.
