From 2 December, "nudify" AI is banned in the EU. And the ban reaches Indian AI companies with EU users.

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 4 November 2026 · 3 min read
View on LinkedIn

The EU's "Digital Omnibus" on AI, published in July, delayed the high-risk rules to 2027–28. But it added a new prohibited practice under Article 5 of the AI Act, and it applies in four weeks:
🔹 AI systems that generate child sexual abuse material or non-consensual intimate content of real people are banned
🔹 Not only where that's the intended purpose ("nudifier" apps), but also where it's a reasonably foreseeable, reproducible outcome and the provider lacks adequate safeguards
🔹 Maximum fine: €35 million or 7% of global turnover

On the same day, the grace period for marking AI output ends. Generative AI tools on the EU market before 2 August 2026 must now mark their output in a machine-readable way (fines up to €15 million or 3%).

Who's caught: providers anywhere whose systems are placed on the EU market or whose output is used in the EU. That includes general image and video generators, not just apps built for abuse.

How India compares:
India's IT Rules amendment (in force 20 February 2026) requires visible labels and permanent metadata on synthetic content, user declarations and verification on big platforms, automated filters against CSAM and non-consensual intimate imagery, and 3-hour takedowns on lawful orders. Criminal law adds IT Act ss. 66E, 67A and 67B, BNS s. 77 and POCSO.

The key difference: India regulates the content and the platforms hosting it. The EU now regulates the tool that creates it.

My take, for India's coming AI law:
1️⃣ Takedowns come too late; once an intimate deepfake spreads, the harm is done
2️⃣ Image and video generators should have a duty to block non-consensual intimate content of real people
3️⃣ Providers should answer for foreseeable misuse they ignored
4️⃣ Align India's labelling with EU marking, so Indian companies don't build twice

For Indian AI companies with EU users (slide 7): map whether your tools can depict real people, red-team for intimate and CSAM outputs, remove "undress" and non-consensual face-swap features, mark output, and document your safeguards.
