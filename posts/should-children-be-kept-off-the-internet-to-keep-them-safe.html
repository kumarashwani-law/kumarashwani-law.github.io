Should children be kept off the internet to keep them safe? The Supreme Court has said no.

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 20 October 2026 · 2 min read
View on LinkedIn

In Just Rights for Children Alliance v. S. Harish (order dated 15 October 2026, Pardiwala & Chandran, JJ.), the Court held that internet access is a fundamental right for every individual, children included, under Article 21 read with Article 19(1)(a).

Its reasoning in one line: "To keep the child away from the internet on account of such failures would be to mistake the victim for the problem."

Key holdings:
🔹 Blanket, age-based bans are disproportionate. They may push children to unregulated platforms and make them less likely to report abuse
🔹 Checking every user's age carries its own risk of sensitive data collection and surveillance
🔹 Protection should target the harm: those who make, share or sell child sexual exploitation and abuse material (CSEAM), and platforms that fail to remove it
🔹 Platforms that ignore POCSO and IT Rules duties cannot rely on safe harbour under Section 79

What the Court wants instead:
✔ A "child mode": private by default, limited contact from unknown adults, moderated ads and content
✔ A National Hash & URL List Database for CSEAM, with at least three trained human analysts making the final assessment
✔ Immediate removal of content that matches the database
✔ Reporting within two steps, acknowledgement within 24 hours, and final decisions by trained humans, not algorithms
✔ Exemplary fines scaled to a platform's size and turnover, and forfeiture of revenue earned from such content behind paywalls
✔ Digital safety education in schools and free counselling for victims

The Centre must report progress by the next hearing on 10 December 2026.

My take: the DPDP angle (slide 7)
Section 9 of the DPDP Act requires verifiable parental consent before processing data of anyone under 18, and bars tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted ads aimed at children, from 13 May 2027. But verifying consent means knowing who is a child, and the Court has warned that checking everyone's age is itself a privacy risk. Platforms will need age checks that collect as little data as possible.

The burden has shifted from children to platforms.
