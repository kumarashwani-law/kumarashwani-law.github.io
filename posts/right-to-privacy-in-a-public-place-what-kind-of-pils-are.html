"Right to privacy in a public place? What kind of PILs are being filed?"

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 15 November 2026 · 3 min read
View on LinkedIn

That was the Centre's response in the Supreme Court on 3 November. Justice Joymalya Bagchi answered with a question of his own: "When I am standing in a public place, what's my reasonable expectation?"

The case: SFLC.in has challenged facial recognition at protest sites, including the 10 October Jantar Mantar protest. It points to Delhi Police's DP-Drishti and Ikshana vans, which allegedly run AI facial recognition on live CCTV feeds. A bench of CJI Surya Kant and Justices Bagchi and V. Mohana issued notice. A similar plea by MP A.A. Rahim on July's protests is pending.

Why the numbers worry (as cited in the petition):
🔹 Police reportedly claimed to identify 2,873 people at July's Jantar Mantar protests
🔹 Of 205 "matches" examined, 25 were already in jail when flagged as present
🔹 An RTI reply reportedly confirms no privacy impact assessment was done
🔹 Organisers' addresses, phone numbers and social media accounts were allegedly collected

The Centre argued that CCTV prevents untoward incidents and protects women's safety. The bench called that "very simplistic" and drew the key line: seeing a face is different from what is later done with the data.

Why public doesn't mean unprotected:
1️⃣ Puttaswamy (2017) attached privacy to the person, not the place
2️⃣ Being seen isn't being identified. Technology turns a face in a crowd into a name in a database
3️⃣ Aggregation matters: the US Supreme Court (Carpenter, 2018) required a warrant for records tracking a person's movements, even in public
4️⃣ Article 19 protects peaceful assembly, and fear of being logged chills it

The gap: no law authorises facial recognition at protests. The Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022 covers biometrics of convicts and arrested persons, not people who simply attend a protest. Phone tapping got safeguards in PUCL (1997); face scanning has none. Telangana's own challenge, S.Q. Masood's PIL, has been pending since notice in 2022.

My take: a statute first. No face matching at peaceful protests without a specific threat and written authorisation. Delete non-matches within days, audit accuracy, and require independent oversight. All six safeguards are on slide 7.

A crowd is not a suspect list.
