The AI gave you a perfect citation. The Supreme Court says check it anyway.

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 18 October 2026
View on LinkedIn

Twice in 2026, the Supreme Court has set aside decisions that relied on AI-generated fake citations.

1️⃣ Pooja Ramesh Singh v. J&K Bank Ltd., 2026 INSC 668 (2 July 2026, Narasimha & Aradhe, JJ.)
The NCLT relied on six judgments that were non-existent, wrongly titled or cited with paragraphs that don't exist. The NCLAT affirmed without checking them. The Supreme Court set both orders aside and held:
• A decision based on fake and hallucinated material "is no decision at all"
• Citing AI-generated precedents without verification is professional misconduct
• Courts and tribunals relying on such material commit a serious lapse
• The Bar Council of India must form a committee to frame guiding principles and disciplinary action
It also made clear that legitimate use of AI in legal practice is not restricted.

2️⃣ Vijay Ghanshyam Gadiya v. Union of India (2 September 2026, Datta & Nagu, JJ.)
A ₹425 crore customs penalty fell because the adjudicating officer's order relied on non-existent cases and misattributed holdings. The matter was sent back to a different officer. The Court's line: "assistance can never be substituted for adjudication."

What this means for advocates:
The Bar Council of India has said it plainly: "The machine neither signs the pleading nor owes duties to the court." The advocate on record is responsible for every citation, and misconduct under Section 35 of the Advocates Act can lead to reprimand, suspension or removal from the roll.

My 7-point check before filing (slide 6):
✔ Pull every judgment from SCC Online, Manupatra or the court's website
✔ Match the title, citation, bench and date
✔ Read the paragraph you cite
✔ Confirm it is still good law
✔ Check sections against the current Bare Act (IPC → BNS, CrPC → BNSS)
✔ Never cite a judgment you haven't read
✔ Keep client-confidential material out of public AI tools

AI is a useful research assistant. It isn't a source of law.
