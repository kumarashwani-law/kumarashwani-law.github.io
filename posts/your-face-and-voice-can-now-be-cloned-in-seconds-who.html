Your face and voice can now be cloned in seconds. Who protects them?

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 19 October 2026
View on LinkedIn

India has no statute on personality rights. Yet in October 2026 alone, High Courts have protected four actors against AI deepfakes:

🔹 Samantha Ruth Prabhu (Bombay HC, 1 Oct): interim protection against deepfakes, morphed images and AI voice clips
🔹 Kangana Ranaut v. Meta (Delhi HC, 8 Oct): Meta to take down deepfakes and fabricated quotes within 72 hours; MeitY and DoT to block sites
🔹 Vijay Deverakonda (Delhi HC): AI-morphed and obscene videos to be removed; domains locked within 72 hours
🔹 Arjun Kapoor (Delhi HC): a final decree, not just an interim order, covering generative AI, deepfakes and face-morphing

Kriti Sanon's suit is listed for 29 October.

How judges are building this law:
1️⃣ Article 21: privacy and dignity (Puttaswamy). In Samantha's case, the Bombay HC said morphed sexual content violates dignity even if no one profits from it
2️⃣ Section 38B, Copyright Act: a performer's moral rights
3️⃣ Passing off and publicity rights: the commercial value of a recognisable identity

What's still unsettled:
• Where parody, satire and political commentary end and misappropriation begins
• Not every name qualifies: Deverakonda was refused an ex parte injunction over the nickname "Rowdy" because secondary meaning wasn't yet shown
• Most orders are interim and ex parte

The bigger question is what happens to people who aren't famous. Their options today (slide 7):
✔ Complain to the platform's grievance officer under the IT Rules
✔ Report at cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930
✔ Seek an injunction based on privacy and dignity, which doesn't depend on fame
✔ Preserve evidence first: URLs, dated screenshots, account names

Judges are writing the law that Parliament hasn't. India needs a statutory right that protects everyone's face and voice, not only celebrities'.
