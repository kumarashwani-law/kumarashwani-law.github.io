Two words changed in India's data protection law this month. Here's why they matter.

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 17 October 2026
View on LinkedIn

On 5 October 2026, MeitY issued the Digital Personal Data Protection (Removal of Difficulties) Order, 2026 under Section 43(1) of the DPDP Act. It was published in the Gazette on 7 October.

The changes are small, but both affect how compliance programmes are written:

1️⃣ Section 9(1): children and persons with disability
The text now reads "personal data of a child or of a person with disability". The added "of" makes the two categories parallel and independent. Verifiable consent from a parent or lawful guardian is still required, exactly as before.

2️⃣ Section 10(2)(c)(ii): Significant Data Fiduciaries
"Periodic audit" becomes "periodic data audit". The SDF audit is about data protection compliance, not a general audit, and audit charters and auditor engagements should say so.

What has not changed:
• No new obligations are created
• The ban on tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising aimed at children stays
• Penalties of up to ₹200 crore (children's data) and ₹150 crore (SDF duties) remain

Sections 9 and 10 commence on 13 May 2027 under the November 2025 commencement notification. For ed-tech platforms, schools, health apps and large platforms, this Order is a useful prompt to:
• Align consent notices and privacy policies with the clarified wording
• Map every flow of children's and PwD data and test verifiable-consent journeys
• Re-label SDF audit scopes and engagement letters as "data audit"
