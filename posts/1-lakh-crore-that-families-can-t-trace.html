₹1 lakh crore that families can't trace

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 17 November 2026 · 3 min read
View on LinkedIn

Today (17 November), the Supreme Court reserved its order on Sucheta Dalal's PIL (W.P.(C) 185/2022). She is asking for one searchable database so that heirs can find money a deceased person left behind.

💰 How much is lying unclaimed?
About ₹1.04 lakh crore, by figures cited in December 2025:
• Bank deposits: ~₹78,000 crore (moved to RBI's DEAF after 10 years of inactivity)
• Insurance: ~₹14,000 crore
• Unpaid dividends: ~₹9,000 crore
• Mutual funds: ~₹3,000 crore

🧩 Why heirs give up
There are five separate portals: UDGAM (banks), MITRA (mutual funds), Bima Bharosa (insurance), IEPF (shares and dividends) and EPFO (PF). Heirs often don't know an asset exists, so they don't know where to search.

⏳ A promise three years late
The government told the Court on affidavit that a centralised database would be ready by July 2023. It still doesn't exist.

⚖️ What each side said today
Petitioner: one search by the deceased person's name should show every account. No effective steps yet on insurance. UDGAM assumes you know which bank held the account.
Centre, RBI and SEBI: 30 banks are on UDGAM, covering about 90% of unclaimed deposits; users can "select all banks"; banks now allow up to four nominees; no directions are needed.

🔐 The privacy question
Who should be able to search a dead person's money? A name-searchable list could help impostors, and the Centre has raised privacy and misuse concerns. The answer is careful design: search unlocked by a death certificate plus the claimant's KYC, results showing only where an asset is held (not the balance), logged searches, and claims still verified by the institution.

✅ What families can do now
1️⃣ Add nominees to every bank account, demat, MF folio, insurance policy and EPF.
2️⃣ Keep an asset list where your family can find it.
3️⃣ Searching for a late relative? Use UDGAM with "all banks", then MITRA and IEPF.
4️⃣ Section 14 of the DPDP Act will let you nominate someone to exercise your data rights after death once it comes into force on 13 May 2027.

My take: a deadline the government set for itself should not need a court order. Whatever the Court orders, it should set a timeline.
