Digital arrest scams and bank liability

In Re: Victims of Digital Arrest Related to Forged Documents (suo motu)

The question: What banks, the RBI and platforms such as WhatsApp must do to prevent digital arrest frauds, and who compensates victims.

Where it stands: On 29 October the Court said the RBI's new rules must make banks compensate victims where a failure of due diligence enabled the fraud, and that it would examine the draft rules before they are notified. It also asked WhatsApp to propose measures. The order uploaded on 7 November lists the case next on 23 November; some earlier reports gave 30 November.

Next23 November 2026
My analysis
  • A "digital arrest" victim sends the money themselves. Until now, that meant the bank could say: not our problem. The Supreme Court has just changed that conversation.

Pending Supreme Court

Children's safety online

Just Rights for Children Alliance v. S. Harish

The question: How to protect children from online sexual exploitation without banning them from the internet.

Where it stands: Order of 15 October 2026: internet access is a fundamental right for children too; blanket age-based bans are disproportionate. The Court directed a child mode, a national hash database, faster takedowns and fines scaled to platform size. The Centre must report progress at the next hearing.

Next10 December 2026
My analysis
  • Should children be kept off the internet to keep them safe? The Supreme Court has said no.

Challenge to the DPDP Act and the RTI amendment

Venkatesh Nayak v. Union of India (W.P.(C) 177/2026) and connected petitions, including the Digital News Publishers Association

The question: Whether s. 44(3), which narrowed the RTI exemption for personal information, and other provisions (ss. 17, 33, 36 and the Board's selection rules) are constitutional, and whether journalism needs an exemption.

Where it stands: Notice issued and no interim stay. The RTI petitions were referred to a larger bench in February 2026. On 27 October the Court issued notice on the news publishers' petition and declined an interim stay without hearing the Union. The matter was listed with the connected petitions on 17 November; the outcome of that hearing has not been reported yet.

NextListed 17 Nov 2026; next date awaited
My analysis
  • Can a privacy law quietly weaken the Right to Information? The Supreme Court hears that question again on 17 November.
  • Can a privacy law stop journalists from doing their job? India's biggest media houses have asked the Supreme Court.

No writ against Meta for blocking an account

Sarthak Bhagat v. Union of India

The question: Can a user challenge Instagram's account block by writ?

Where it stands: 27 October 2026: writ not maintainable against Meta; restoration must be sought by civil suit. The Grievance Appellate Committee was directed to decide the appeal quickly.

Decided27 October 2026
My analysis
  • Instagram blocked your account. Can you go straight to the High Court? The Delhi High Court says no, at least not by writ.
Decided Punjab & Haryana High Court

User must get a copy of a takedown notice

Haryana 24 News Network v. Union of India

The question: Was an SP's s. 79(3)(b) notice that got a news page blocked valid?

Where it stands: Reported 20 October 2026: the notice was without jurisdiction (Rule 3(1)(d) needs a DIG-rank officer), a copy must go to the user, and the page was ordered restored.

Decided20 October 2026
My analysis
  • Your page is blocked. You don't know who ordered it, or why. How do you challenge that?

The question: Can Instagram suspend a creator's account over copyright complaints without a court order?
