Digital arrest scams | Banks must compensate victims where due diligence fails

Ashwani KumarAdvocate · 29 October 2026 · Bench: Surya Kant CJI, Joymalya Bagchi J, V. Mohana J

On 29 October, a Bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana said the RBI's proposed anti-cyber-fraud regulations must require financial institutions to compensate victims where a fraud results from a failure of due diligence. The compensation must be at least equal to the customer's loss.

The Court said it would examine the draft regulations before they are notified. The RBI proposed notifying them by 30 November and bringing them into operation by 31 December.

The Court directed WhatsApp and other intermediaries to set out the measures they propose to prevent digital arrest scams, and to share them at the same time with the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C). Counsel for WhatsApp said the platform had acted against about 9,400 accounts.

The RBI was directed to file a list of all authorised financial institutions, and law enforcement agencies to report the status of digital arrest FIRs. The order was uploaded on 7 November, and the case was listed next on 23 November.
