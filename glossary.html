Glossary

Short, plain-language definitions of the legal terms that come up in my writing. On articles and guides, terms in this list are underlined with dots: tap or hover to see the definition.

BCDFGIKLNPSTW
Blocking order
An order under Section 69A of the IT Act directing a platform or internet provider to block public access to online content, on grounds such as sovereignty, security of the State or public order. The procedure is set by the 2009 Blocking Rules, which keep most orders confidential.Read more
Consent manager
Under the DPDP Act, a platform registered with the Data Protection Board through which people can give, manage, review and withdraw their consent across many businesses.Read more
Data fiduciary
Under the DPDP Act, the person or business that decides why and how personal data is processed, alone or with others. It carries most of the Act's duties.Read more
Data principal
Under the DPDP Act, the individual the personal data is about. For a child, it includes the parent or lawful guardian.Read more
Data processor
Under the DPDP Act, anyone who processes personal data on behalf of a data fiduciary, such as a cloud or payroll vendor. The fiduciary stays responsible for it.Read more
Data Protection Board
The body set up under the DPDP Act to inquire into personal data breaches and other violations, and to impose penalties of up to ₹250 crore.Read more
Deepfake
Audio, video or images made or altered with AI so that a real person appears to say or do something they never did.Read more
Digital arrest
A scam, not a legal procedure. Fraudsters posing as police or other officials keep a victim on a video call, claim they are 'under arrest', and pressure them into transferring money. No Indian law allows anyone to be arrested over a video call.Read more
DPIA (Data Protection Impact Assessment)
A documented assessment of the risks a type of data processing poses to people's rights, and how those risks are reduced. Periodic DPIAs are mandatory for Significant Data Fiduciaries under the DPDP Act.Read more
Facial recognition technology (FRT)
Software that identifies or verifies people by comparing images of their faces with a database. Its use by police raises questions of accuracy, consent and proportionality under the right to privacy.Read more
Fair dealing
Exceptions in Section 52 of the Copyright Act, 1957 that allow certain uses of a work without permission, such as private or personal use including research, criticism or review, and reporting current events.Read more
Fair use
The US copyright doctrine that allows some unlicensed uses, decided by weighing four factors: the purpose of the use, the nature of the work, how much is used, and the effect on the market for the original.Read more
Grievance Appellate Committee
A government-appointed committee under the IT Rules, 2021 that hears users' appeals against decisions of a social media platform's grievance officer, such as a refusal to restore content or an account.Read more
Interim injunction
A temporary court order that stops a party from doing something until the case is finally decided. Courts grant it if there is a prima facie case, the balance of convenience favours it, and refusing it would cause irreparable harm.
Intermediary
Under Section 2(1)(w) of the IT Act, anyone who receives, stores or transmits electronic records for others, or provides a service for them: telecom and internet providers, search engines, marketplaces and social media platforms.Read more
Keyword advertising
Ads that appear when someone searches a word or phrase the advertiser has bid on, sometimes a competitor's trademark. Indian courts treat this as 'use' of the mark, but it infringes only if it confuses buyers or takes unfair advantage of the mark.Read more
Legitimate use
Situations listed in Section 7 of the DPDP Act where personal data can be processed without consent, for example data a person gave voluntarily for a stated purpose, employment purposes, medical emergencies and certain State functions.Read more
Natural justice
Basic fairness rules every decision-maker must follow: hear the other side before deciding (audi alteram partem), no one should judge their own cause, and decisions should give reasons.Read more
Passing off
A legal action against misrepresenting that your goods, services or endorsement are connected with someone else, in a way that damages their goodwill. It protects unregistered marks and, increasingly, a person's persona.Read more
Personal data breach
Under the DPDP Act, any unauthorised processing, accidental disclosure, sharing, alteration or loss of access to personal data that compromises its confidentiality, integrity or availability. It must be reported to the Board and to affected people.Read more
Personality rights
The right to control commercial use of your name, image, likeness, voice and other identifiable traits. Indian courts protect it as part of the right to privacy under Article 21 and through passing off.Read more
Prima facie
Latin for 'at first sight'. A court's preliminary view on the material before it, usually at the interim stage, which can change at the final hearing.
Safe harbour
The protection in Section 79 of the IT Act that shields intermediaries from liability for content posted by others, as long as they follow due diligence rules and act when they get actual knowledge through a court order or government notice.Read more
Significant Data Fiduciary
A data fiduciary notified by the government because of the volume and sensitivity of data it processes or the risks involved. It must appoint a Data Protection Officer in India, an independent data auditor, and carry out periodic DPIAs.Read more
Suo motu
Latin for 'on its own motion'. A case a court starts by itself, without anyone filing a petition, usually on a matter of public importance.
Text and data mining (TDM)
Automated analysis of large volumes of text or data, as in AI training. EU law allows it for commercial purposes unless the rights holder has opted out in a machine-readable way.Read more
Well-known trademark
A mark so widely recognised by the relevant public that the Trade Marks Act protects it even against use on unrelated goods or services, and against uses that take unfair advantage of its reputation.Read more
