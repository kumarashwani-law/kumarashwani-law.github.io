Digital arrest scams and bank liability

In Re: Victims of Digital Arrest Related to Forged Documents (suo motu)

Court
Supreme Court
Case number
SMW (Crl.) 3/2025
Bench
Surya Kant CJI, Joymalya Bagchi J, V. Mohana J
Status
Pending
Next hearing
Listed 23 Nov 2026; next date awaited
Last updated
25 November 2026

Key issues

  1. What must banks and the RBI do to stop money moving to fraud accounts?
  2. When must a bank compensate a victim who was tricked into sending the money?
  3. What must WhatsApp and other platforms do to prevent these scams?

Where it stands

On 29 October the Court said the RBI's new rules must make banks compensate victims where a failure of due diligence enabled the fraud, and that it would examine the draft rules before they are notified. It also asked WhatsApp to propose measures. The order was uploaded on 7 November. The case was listed again on 23 November; the outcome has not been reported yet.

Timeline

  3. 4 August 2026The RBI is directed to adopt, within four weeks, an SOP for banks to place temporary debit holds on accounts linked to cyber fraud.
  4. 9 February 2026The Court calls the frauds "robbery or dacoity" and asks the Centre to draft SOPs with the RBI and others, and to work out a compensation framework.
  5. 1 December 2025The Court orders a pan-India CBI probe, including into the alleged role of bank officials.
  6. 17 October 2025The Supreme Court takes suo motu cognisance on a complaint by a senior-citizen couple from Ambala, Haryana.
