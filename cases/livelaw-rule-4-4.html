Automated blocking of news reports (Rule 4(4), IT Rules)

Live Law Media Pvt. Ltd. & Anr. v. Union of India & Ors.

Court
Delhi High Court
Case number
Writ petition dated 4 November 2026
Bench
Nitin Wasudeo Sambre ACJ, Sanjay Sharma J
Status
Pending
Next hearing
18 January 2027
Last updated
25 November 2026

Key issues

  1. Is the requirement in Rule 4(4) to automatically restrict content "exactly identical" to content already removed within the IT Act's rule-making power and consistent with Articles 14, 19 and 21?
  2. Can platforms use automated blocking against news and court reporting without a court order, a reasoned intimation or a Section 69A direction given to the publisher?
  3. Are the unpublished MeitY advisories of 8 and 10 October 2026 a valid basis for restricting content?

Where it stands

LiveLaw's Instagram reels reporting Supreme Court hearings were restricted in India, two of them within about 15 minutes of posting. On 19 November 2026 a Bench of Acting Chief Justice Nitin Wasudeo Sambre and Justice Sanjay Sharma issued notice to the Union and Meta, with four weeks to reply. Interim relief was not pressed.

Timeline

  1. 19 November 2026Notice issued to the Union and Meta; four weeks to reply; listed on 18 January 2027.
  2. 30 October 2026A third reel, on a hearing about the Transgender Persons Amendment Act, 2026, is restricted on publication.
  3. 29 October 2026A second reel, on the SIR challenges, is restricted within about 15 minutes and not restored.
  4. 27 October 2026LiveLaw's reel on the Election Commission's SIR affidavit is restricted within about 15 minutes; restored that night.
  5. 10 February 2026IT Rules amendment (G.S.R. 120(E)) makes automated filtering under Rule 4(4) mandatory and cuts takedown time from 36 hours to 3; in force 20 February 2026.
